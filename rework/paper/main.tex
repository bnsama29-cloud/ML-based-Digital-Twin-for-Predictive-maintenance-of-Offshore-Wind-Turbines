\begin{abstract}
Offshore wind turbines are expensive to visit and are watched mostly through 10-minute SCADA streams. This paper describes one pipeline that turns those streams into an anomaly alarm, a subsystem attribution, a health index, a time-to-threshold estimate with an uncertainty band, and a timestamped 3D playback. An autoencoder is trained only on normal-status training rows of each CARE-to-Compare farm-C dataset and evaluated on all 58 farm-C events (26 anomalies, 32 normal periods) against Isolation Forest, PCA and a per-channel 3$\sigma$ rule. With a threshold fixed in advance, the autoencoder raised an alarm in \PH{x} of 26 fault events and in \PH{y} of 32 normal periods, with a mean ROC-AUC of \PH{z} over three seeds. Sensor errors are grouped by the published sensor descriptions into ten plant areas, and the top-ranked area matched the operator's written root cause in \PH{p}\% of \PH{n} labelled events, against \PH{q}\% for always naming the most common area. On a cooling-water valve fault, a normal-behaviour model of generator cooling-water temperature drifted by \PH{d}~$^\circ$C from its expected value during the event. The data hold no component replacement dates, so the time-to-threshold figure is a planning estimate, not a calibrated remaining-useful-life forecast.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Offshore plants often produce more energy than onshore machines of similar rating, but a repair visit needs a vessel, a weather window, and spare parts \cite{r5,r8}. Two common policies sit at opposite extremes. Reactive repair waits for a stop. Calendar-based preventive repair visits the machine whether it needs work or not \cite{r3,r5}. A middle path is to read the SCADA archive, mark unusual behaviour early, and only then place a work order.

Most SCADA papers stop at one of those steps. Threshold rules and linear models miss coupled non-linear channels \cite{r3,r9}. Deep models find unusual rows but rarely name a plant area \cite{r2,r4,r11}. Autoencoders give a reconstruction error that can stand in for asset health \cite{r10}, yet that score is seldom turned into a maintenance date or shown on a plant model \cite{r7,r8}. Full physics twins exist, but they need models and instrumentation that a public SCADA dump does not provide \cite{r7,r14}.

An earlier version of this work trained on a single file that already contained the fault, reported no detection rates and compared against nothing. This version fixes those three gaps. The pipeline in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} does the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Clean and scale each 10-minute SCADA matrix with statistics taken from normal training rows only.
\item Train an autoencoder on those rows and score every row by reconstruction error.
\item Raise an alarm when unusual rows persist, using the counter that ships with the benchmark \cite{r12}.
\item Group channel errors into ten plant areas named from the sensor descriptions.
\item Turn the error into a health index (HI) and read a causal HI slope as time to a chosen floor, with a 95\% band.
\item Replay measured SCADA values and model outputs on a simplified Unity turbine.
\end{enumerate}
The contributions are kept to what the public data can support:
\begin{itemize}
\item an evaluation on all 58 farm-C events with precision, recall, false-alarm rate and three baselines, repeated over three seeds;
\item a check of subsystem attribution against the root causes that operators wrote into the dataset;
\item a time-to-threshold estimate with an uncertainty band, plus a sensitivity study of the maintenance gate instead of hand-picked weights;
\item a time-indexed 3D playback layer, not a closed-loop or physics twin.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Physics-based condition models stay close to component equations, but they need parameters that anonymized SCADA files do not publish \cite{r3,r6,r9}. Vibration-based bearing and gearbox diagnostics are strong when those sensors exist \cite{r1}; the farm-C data used here are SCADA only. Statistical limits on single tags struggle once many channels move together \cite{r3}. Deep sequence models improve detection on vibration and SCADA traces \cite{r2,r4}, and autoencoders treat a large reconstruction error as a sign that the current row does not look like the rows seen in training \cite{r10,r11}. Those detectors seldom add a subsystem name or a work-order date.

Digital-twin surveys in wind energy rank implementations from a standalone 3D model up to an autonomous plant \cite{r7,r8}. Structural twins for towers and floating platforms estimate remaining life from load histories \cite{r14,r15}. The Unity scene in this paper sits lower on that scale: it is a descriptive and diagnostic playback of SCADA-derived scores.

Explainable maintenance surveys ask for a chain that a technician can inspect \cite{r3}. The design here follows that request in a simple way: the anomaly score is a mean squared residual, the cause list is a ratio of those residuals inside named groups, and the planning date is a slope on the health index. No claim is made that this chain replaces a physics remaining-life model.

\section{Dataset}
CARE-to-Compare releases 95 SCADA datasets from three farms \cite{r12}. Farm C is an offshore site in Germany with 957 columns per row at a 10-minute step. Each of its 58 datasets covers one turbine and splits into a training year and a prediction window. The prediction window holds either a labelled anomaly event (26 datasets) or a normal period (32 datasets). Every row carries a status code; codes 0 (normal production) and 2 (idling) count as normal operation. Sensor names are anonymized, but the release includes a plain-language description for each sensor (for example ``cooling water temp.\ generator outlet 1'') and, for most anomaly events, a short operator note on the root cause. Time stamps are shifted so that files open in 2022; plots use the shifted calendar.

Three leakage rules were set before any result was seen. Models are fitted only on training-year rows with normal status; the last 15\% of those rows form a validation slice used for early stopping and thresholds and nothing else. Row identifiers, asset identifiers and the status code itself are removed from the inputs, because the status code partly encodes the label. Imputation medians, means and standard deviations come from the fitting rows and are reused unchanged on the prediction window.

Angle channels (wind direction, which wraps at 360$^\circ$), channels with more than 30\% missing values in training and channels that are constant in training are dropped, leaving \PH{min--max} channels per dataset. Short gaps are forward-filled for up to one hour, remaining gaps take the training median, and each channel is standardized, $z=(x-\mu)/\sigma$, then clipped to $\pm10$.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Autoencoder scoring and alarms}
Let $\mathbf{x}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^d$ be the standardized sensor vector at time $t$. A fully connected autoencoder with widths $d\!\to\!256\!\to\!64\!\to\!16\!\to\!64\!\to\!256\!\to\!d$ rebuilds that vector. Hidden layers use ReLU. The output layer is linear, because standardized inputs are unbounded and a sigmoid head cannot reproduce negative values; a sigmoid variant is kept only as an ablation. The anomaly score is
\begin{equation}
e(t)=\frac{1}{d}\lVert \mathbf{x}(t)-\hat{\mathbf{x}}(t)\rVert_2^2 .
\end{equation}
Training uses Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$), batches of 256 and at most 40 epochs, stopping when the validation loss has not improved for five epochs. Three seeds are run per dataset (Table~\ref{tab:settings}).

A row is flagged when $e(t)$ exceeds the 99th percentile of validation scores. Single flagged rows are common in any year of SCADA, so an alarm needs persistence. The benchmark's counter \cite{r12} is used as published: it rises by one on a flagged normal-status row, falls by one (not below zero) on a clean normal-status row, and holds on other statuses. An alarm is raised when it reaches 72, i.e.\ 12~h of net abnormal behaviour. An anomaly event counts as detected if an alarm occurs inside its labelled window; a normal dataset counts as a false alarm if an alarm occurs anywhere in its prediction window.

\subsection{Baselines}
Three baselines see exactly the same rows and thresholds: Isolation Forest with 200 trees \cite{r16}; PCA keeping 95\% of the training variance, scored by reconstruction error; and a rule that scores each row by the fraction of channels lying more than three standard deviations from their training mean.

\subsection{Subsystem attribution}
Each channel is assigned to one of ten plant areas by keywords in its published description: pitch and hub, yaw, hydraulics and brakes, cooling, gearbox, generator, rotor and drivetrain, electrical, nacelle and tower, and environment. Rules are applied in a fixed order so that, for example, ``generator cooling air temperature'' falls under cooling and ``axis 1 cooling element temperature'' under pitch. The assignment is a heuristic, not an OEM schedule.

Large groups would win any comparison based on summed error, so each channel's error over the event window is first divided by its own mean error on the validation slice, and the group score is the mean of those ratios,
\begin{equation}
C_k=\frac{1}{|S_k|}\sum_{i\in S_k}\frac{\bar e_i^{\,\mathrm{event}}}{\bar e_i^{\,\mathrm{val}}} .
\end{equation}
The summed form used in our earlier draft is also reported for comparison. For ground truth, the operator notes in the dataset were read and each anomaly event was given one or more plant areas by hand; \PH{k} notes that only mention communication faults or an unknown standstill were left out. Attribution is scored as top-1 and top-3 hit rates and compared with two floors: always naming the most common area, and a random ranking.

\subsection{Health index and time to threshold}
The score is smoothed with a causal exponential average (span 6~h), giving $\tilde e(t)$. With $q_{50}$ and $q_{99}$ the median and 99th percentile of $\tilde e$ on the validation slice,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{HI}(t)=\left(1+\frac{\max(0,\tilde e(t)-q_{50})}{q_{99}-q_{50}}\right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
HI equals one at typical healthy error, 0.5 at the alarm level, and falls towards zero as error grows. Unlike min--max scaling over the whole file, it uses no future data.

The slope $s(t)$ is an ordinary least-squares fit of HI on time over the previous 24~h only. The time to a floor $\mathrm{HI}_{th}=0.2$ is
\begin{equation}
\widehat T(t)=\frac{\mathrm{HI}(t)-\mathrm{HI}_{th}}{-s(t)},\qquad s(t)<0,
\end{equation}
and is treated as steady (capped at 720~h) when the slope is not negative. Replacing $s$ with $s\pm1.96\,\mathrm{SE}(s)$ gives a 95\% band. The band describes how noisy the recent slope is; it does not cover the larger error of assuming that the slope will continue.

\subsection{Maintenance gate}
A work order is suggested when an alarm is active and $\widehat T$ falls below a gate $G$, with the visit placed a buffer $B$ earlier. The earlier draft fixed $G=168$~h, $B=24$~h and weighted subsystems by hand. Neither choice can be fitted without a cost model or maintenance log, so the subsystem weights are dropped and $G\in\{72,168,336\}$~h and $B\in\{12,24,48\}$~h are reported as a sensitivity table instead.

\subsection{Temperature normal-behaviour model}
A second, supervised check predicts two thermal channels from operating conditions: rotor bearing temperature and generator cooling-water outlet temperature. Inputs are rotor speed, grid active power, ambient temperature and wind speed (farm C has no humidity channel). A histogram gradient-boosting regressor is trained on the normal fitting rows of dataset 44 and compared with linear regression and a constant-mean floor. It is tested on the validation slice of the same turbine (later in time) and on the training year of another turbine (dataset 61). Confidence intervals come from a bootstrap that resamples whole days. On the fault window, the residual between measured and expected temperature is examined directly.

\subsection{Robustness to noise and missing data}
To probe sensor noise and dropouts, the prediction window of dataset 44 is corrupted after scaling, either with Gaussian noise ($\sigma=0.1,0.3,0.5$ in standardized units) or by replacing 5\% or 20\% of entries with the training median, and rescored with the unchanged model and threshold.

\subsection{Playback model}
A simplified rotor, nacelle and tower were built in Unity. A loader reads a time-indexed CSV of measured wind speed, rotor speed, active power, gearbox oil, rotor bearing and cooling-water temperatures, together with the anomaly score, alarm state, HI, $\widehat T$ and its band. A slider steps through the samples. The scene does not solve structural dynamics, does not write set-points back to a turbine and does not ingest a live stream; in the ranking used by wind-twin surveys \cite{r7} it is a descriptive and diagnostic playback.

\section{Limits}
\textit{Life figure.} $\widehat T$ is a short-horizon slope to an HI floor. CARE-to-Compare does not publish component replacement times, so no remaining-useful-life error can be computed. The band covers slope noise only.

\textit{Ground truth for attribution.} Plant areas were assigned to operator notes by the authors. The notes are brief, several events involve more than one area, and pitch faults dominate, so the small number of events limits what the hit rates can show.

\textit{Gate and floor.} $\mathrm{HI}_{th}=0.2$, the gate and the buffer are planning constants. Table~\ref{tab:gate} shows their effect but does not choose them.

\textit{One farm.} All results come from farm C. Farms A and B have different sensor sets and were not tested.

\textit{Offshore practice.} Communication loss, vessel delay and weather lock-outs sit outside the CSV. The robustness test covers noise and dropouts in the signal only.

\textit{Playback twin.} The Unity scene reads a CSV. It does not close a loop with the plant and does not host a physics model.

\section{Conclusion}
An autoencoder trained on normal training rows of each CARE-to-Compare farm-C dataset was evaluated on all 58 events with fixed thresholds, three seeds and three baselines. \PH{One sentence on detection and false alarms from Table~\ref{tab:detection}.} Ranking plant areas by size-normalised error \PH{did / did not} beat the simplest floor when checked against operator notes. On the cooling-valve fault of dataset 44, the cooling group ranked \PH{rank} and the cooling-water temperature model drifted by \PH{d}~$^\circ$C. A causal slope of the health index gives a time-to-threshold estimate with a band, which is a planning aid and not a fitted remaining-life model. The next steps are testing on farms A and B, and fitting the gate to a real maintenance log.
